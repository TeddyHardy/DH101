## 3. Process Documentation

For this project, I chose Track B and used ChatGPT’s image generator. I set up the generator by opening a new chat and uploading a photo of Denison’s campus that I found online. This photo became my base image, and every prompt I gave the generator changed one part of it. I asked the generator to change things such as its art style, its time period, and what could be added to the scene.

The main parameters I controlled were the wording of each prompt, and how much freedom I gave the generator. In my first few prompts, I kept it very specific, not giving AI much freedom. I asked it things like “turn it into an oil painting,” and the AI followed this instruction closely. As I kept going, the AI got sidetracked and added details I never told it to add. In one of my prompts, I never told it to add a basketball court into the image, but it did so anyway. This showed me that small changes in my wording led to bigger changes in the generator’s responses.

One of the biggest challenges I encountered was keeping the image Denison themed. When I asked the AI to make the campus look like the year 4005, it took the AI several attempts to keep it school themed. It kept removing the school aspect completely.

For my last prompt, prompt #20, I wanted to give the AI complete freedom to add whatever it wanted. I wanted to see if it had learned to keep the school in the image or if it would remove it once again. It decided to keep the school theme of the image, but added a floating whale, something I never would have chosen myself.

In total, I generated 20 images. Thirteen kept Denison’s campus as the theme, and I rejected the other seven because they ignored my instructions.

## 4. Artist Statement

When AI can generate endlessly, creativity shifts from making images to making decisions. ChatGPT could create a new version of Denison in seconds, but could not decide what version was worth keeping in later generations.

I made creative decisions at specific moments. I chose the base image of Denison’s campus, and decided to turn it into an oil painting, then a version of Denison in the year 4005. When the AI got sidetracked, I rewrote my next prompt to pull it back on track. I then chose what 3 images to keep, and which to reject.

If someone were to see my images without explanation, I think they would be able to tell a human selected them. All three images I chose kept Denison’s campus recognizable to anyone who has seen it before, while the rejected images left some confusion on whether it included Denison or not.

Pure AI creativity would be the machine generating images without me giving it prompts. When it ignored my instructions, it got sidetracked, and the audience would have a hard time finding a recurring theme throughout the images. Pure human creativity would be painting the campus myself. My work fell in the middle as I had the AI make the images, but I gave it the instructions to do so.

I did feel overwhelmed at times, because every prompt kept generating something different. Whenever the AI generated an image without the school theme, I knew that I would have to make my next prompt even stronger so it wouldn’t get even more sidetracked. I knew to stop after 20 images, as I had three strong ones that each showed something different. Generating more would not have added anything new. I decided what images were “good” based on whether or not it followed my idea and instructions. I wanted each image to keep Denison recognizable, but wanted the generator to surprise me with what it could add.
