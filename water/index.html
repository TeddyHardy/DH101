The Water Cost of the Cloud

Every AI answer runs on servers that get hot. Many data centers cool those servers with water, often millions of gallons a day, and some of the newest are being built in places already running dry.

Why it matters

The pattern is clear: data centers go where land, power and tax breaks are cheap, and water often comes second. In Chile and Uruguay, residents protested Google's plans during record droughts, and a Chilean court partly reversed Google's permit. In Querétaro, Mexico, families get water every few days while new data centers receive water rights. In Iowa and Oregon, the public learned how much water was going to data centers only after journalists pushed for the records. The cloud is not weightless. It drinks.
